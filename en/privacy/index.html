Privacy Policy

Updated: TODO publication date

Tarocchi is a tarot deck on your phone. The questions you ask the deck are personal, so this page says plainly what the app knows, where it lives and who can see it.

IIn short

  • The text of your question goes to Anthropic’s Claude model only to check the question and write the reading — without your name and without your past spreads.
  • Analytics are our own: only the app’s owner sees the events. There are no third-party analytics or ads, and no data is sold.
  • Your IP address is not stored for analytics — only the country is taken from it. At sign-in the address is kept for up to an hour to prevent abuse.
  • Deleting your account wipes everything: the data on the server and the diary on the phone.

IIWho is responsible for the data

The Tarocchi app and this site are run by Ilya Manenkov, an independent developer. Contact: flyyyxxxy@gmail.com.

Questions and readings

The question you write to the deck is stored on your phone and in a copy on our server, so the diary isn’t lost and can move to a new phone. To check the question, suggest a layout and write the reading, the server sends Anthropic’s Claude model the minimum: the question text, the spread, the cards and the language. A question written with your finger is first read by the model from its drawing (see “Handwriting”). No name, no identifiers, no past spreads. We don’t use questions to train anything and never show them to other people.

The text of questions and readings never goes into server logs, error messages or analytics. Only the server and the app’s owner have access to the database.

Analytics

The app sends events to our server: which screens are opened and which actions are taken (for example, “spread started”), the app and iOS version, phone model, device language and region, App Store storefront and country. The country is looked up from the IP address in a local DB-IP Lite database; the address itself is not saved. The text of questions, readings, notes and searches never goes into events.

Analytics help us understand what works in the app and what doesn’t. They are always on; there is no separate switch. Events are linked to your account, but they are not used to track you across apps and websites and are not shared with anyone — which is why Apple’s tracking prompt does not appear.

Crashes

If the app crashes or hits an error, a report goes to our server on the next launch: where the error happened, the version, the device. It contains nothing you wrote. Apple may also share its own crash reports with developers if you allow that in iPhone settings.

Handwriting

A question written with your finger is read by the Claude model (Anthropic): when you place the question under the deck, the phone sends our server a drawing of the strokes — a plain image with no colour or background — and the server passes it to the model together with the question check. What comes back is the line as read, and you can correct it. The server does not keep the drawing and does not write it to logs or analytics. The strokes themselves stay with the question, so the envelope and the diary keep your own handwriting.

Sign in with Apple

Your account starts out anonymous. If you sign in with Apple, the server stores the identifier Apple provides — that is how your diary finds you on a new phone. The server does not keep your name or email from Apple.

Subscription

Purchases go through the App Store. The server learns your subscription status from RevenueCat, a service that verifies purchases. The app never sees your payment details.

VIHow long it is kept

  • Diary, questions and readings — until you delete them or delete your account.
  • A burned question is removed from the phone and the server immediately and for good, handwriting included. It disappears from database backups as they rotate — within 30 days.
  • Raw analytics events — 25 months, then deleted. Aggregate numbers (for example, how many spreads were made in a day) are kept indefinitely and are not linked to anyone.
  • The print of your 79th card — on the server until the account is deleted.
  • Crash reports and the notification token — crash reports for 90 days; the notification token is deleted after 60 days without activity.
  • IP address at sign-in — up to one hour, to prevent abuse.

VIIWhat you can do in the app

  • Burn a question — in the diary. The text and handwriting burn; the date and cards stay.
  • Delete the diary — in Settings.
  • Delete your account — Settings → Account → Delete account. Everything is wiped: the data on the server, the link to your Apple ID and to purchases in RevenueCat, the diary and settings on this phone. Analytics events lose their link to you. This cannot be undone. There is no signing out without deleting: the account and the diary live together.
  • Deleting the account does not cancel a subscription — that is done in your Apple ID settings.
  • To ask what data we hold about you, or to ask us to delete it, write to flyyyxxxy@gmail.com.
